\begin{abstract}
This note derives the first-order distorted-wave Born approximation (DWBA)
used by orGUI from the complex X-ray refractive index through the far-field
electric field.  It fixes the dimensional prefactors in the relation between
atomic scattering factors and the refractive index, states a self-consistent
Green-function sign convention, and identifies the quantities returned by
the bulk and specular DWBA APIs.  For a semi-infinite bulk, the atomic density
must be measured relative to the homogeneous density already used to generate
the reference Fresnel field.  On the specular rod this produces an additional
term proportional to $1/(\ii Q_z)$.  The term vanishes off-specular because a
laterally homogeneous reference has no Fourier component at nonzero in-plane
momentum transfer.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and conventions}

The purpose of this note is to establish the normalization needed before the
bulk DWBA structure factor can be combined coherently with the reference
specular reflection amplitude.  The derivation concerns a nonmagnetic,
isotropic, linear, reciprocal medium.  Magnetic and tensor-resonant scattering
require a corresponding tensor susceptibility and are outside the present
scope.

We use the crystallographic space-time convention
\begin{equation}
  \bm{E}(\bm{r},t)\propto
  \exp\!\left[\ii\left(\omega t-\bm{k}\mathbin{\cdot}\bm{r}\right)\right],
  \label{eq:space-time-convention}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
  \kzero=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda},
  \qquad
  \bm{Q}=\bm{k}_f-\bm{k}_i.
  \label{eq:k0-and-Q}
\end{equation}
The Fourier transform used for a density $g(\bm r)$ is
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde g(\bm Q)
  =\int g(\bm r)\exp(\ii\bm Q\mathbin{\cdot}\bm r)\,\dd^3r.
  \label{eq:fourier-convention}
\end{equation}
The surface normal is the $z$ direction.  Vacuum lies at $z>0$, and the bulk
extends toward negative $z$.  The normal wavevectors in orGUI follow the
Renaud sign convention
\begin{equation}
  k_{z,j}=-\kzero
  \sqrt{n_j^2-n_0^2\cos^2\alpha},
  \qquad
  \operatorname{Re}k_{z,j}\leq 0,
  \quad
  \operatorname{Im}k_{z,j}\geq 0.
  \label{eq:renaud-kz}
\end{equation}
The branch choice makes waves propagating or decaying into the sample behave
correctly with Eq.~\eqref{eq:space-time-convention}.

All electric-field amplitudes in the following equations are normalized to a
unit incident plane-wave amplitude unless stated otherwise.  A selected
incident and analyzed exit polarization are treated explicitly.  The scalar
formulae are recovered by replacing the vector contraction by ordinary
multiplication.

\section{Refractive index and scattering-factor density}

\subsection{Atomic scattering factors}

For atom $j$, the complex X-ray scattering factor is written
\begin{equation}
  f_j(\bm Q,\lambda)
  =f_j^0(\bm Q)+f'_j(\lambda)+\ii f''_j(\lambda).
  \label{eq:atomic-factor}
\end{equation}
Here $f_j^0$ is the normal, momentum-dependent atomic form factor, while
$f'_j$ and $f''_j$ are the energy-dependent anomalous dispersion terms.  The
exact quantity $f_j^0(\bm 0)$ equals the number of electrons of the neutral
atom when ionic corrections are absent.  orGUI evaluates the normal factor
with the five-Gaussian Waasmaier--Kirfel representation
\begin{equation}
  f_{j,\mathrm{WK}}^0(Q^2)
  =\sum_{m=1}^{5}a_{jm}
   \exp\!\left(-\frac{b_{jm}Q^2}{16\pi^2}\right)+c_j.
  \label{eq:waasmaier-kirfel}
\end{equation}
The stored exponents already contain the factor $1/(16\pi^2)$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:waasmaier-kirfel} is an entire function of $Q^2$ and is therefore
used without modification for the generally complex channel values
$Q^2=Q_x^2+Q_y^2+Q_z^2$ in DWBA.  The anomalous contribution
$f'_j+\ii f''_j$ depends on photon energy but not on the DWBA channel.

At zero transfer the implemented forward factor is consequently
\begin{equation}
  f_{j,\mathrm{opt}}
  =f_{j,\mathrm{WK}}^0(0)+f'_j+\ii f''_j
  =\sum_{m=1}^{5}a_{jm}+c_j+f'_j+\ii f''_j.
  \label{eq:implemented-forward-factor}
\end{equation}
This fitted zero-transfer value can differ slightly from the exact neutral
electron count $Z_j$.  orGUI deliberately does not replace it by $Z_j$ in
the optical calculation: the layered refractive-index profile, homogeneous
bulk reference, kinematical structure factor, and DWBA kernel all use the
same Waasmaier--Kirfel limit.  Ionic species use their ionic
Waasmaier--Kirfel coefficients rather than a neutral-atom substitution.

It is useful to define a complex scattering-factor density
\begin{equation}
  \rho_f(\bm r)
  =\sum_j o_j\,
  \mathcal F^{-1}\!\left[f_j(\bm Q,\lambda)\right]
  (\bm r-\bm r_j),
  \label{eq:scattering-density}
\end{equation}
where $o_j$ is the occupancy and $\bm r_j$ is the atomic position.  The units
of $\rho_f$ are electrons per cubic Angstrom when the scattering factors are
expressed in electron units.  Thermal disorder is applied in reciprocal
space through the Debye--Waller factor and therefore need not be included in
the real-space definition in Eq.~\eqref{eq:scattering-density}.

\subsection{Correct refractive-index prefactor}

Let $r_e$ denote the classical electron radius,
\begin{equation}
  r_e=2.8179403262\times10^{-5}\ \text{Angstrom}.
\end{equation}
For the convention in Eq.~\eqref{eq:space-time-convention}, the refractive
index is
\begin{equation}
  n(\bm r)
  =1-\frac{2\pi r_e}{\kzero^2}\rho_f(\bm r).
  \label{eq:index-from-density}
\end{equation}
For a homogeneous atomic number density $\rho_a$, this becomes
\begin{equation}
  n
  =1-\delta-\ii\beta
  =1-\frac{2\pi r_e\rho_a}{\kzero^2}
  \left[f_{\mathrm{WK}}^0(0)+f'+\ii f''\right].
  \label{eq:index-homogeneous}
\end{equation}
The factor $2\pi r_e\rho_a/\kzero^2$ is dimensionless.  A coefficient
proportional to $r_e\rho_a/\kzero$ would have dimensions of inverse length and
cannot be a refractive-index decrement.

Because $|n-1|\ll 1$ for hard X-rays, terms quadratic in the susceptibility
may be neglected:
\begin{equation}
  n^2(\bm r)
  \simeq 1-\frac{4\pi r_e}{\kzero^2}\rho_f(\bm r).
  \label{eq:index-squared}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Homogeneous bulk reference and perturbation}

For the simple bulk problem, the unperturbed reference medium is vacuum above
a homogeneous semi-infinite substrate:
\begin{equation}
  n_0(z)=
  \begin{cases}
    1, & z>0,\\[1mm]
    1-\delta_0-\ii\beta_0, & z<0.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:reference-index}
\end{equation}
The equivalent homogeneous complex scattering-factor density is
\begin{equation}
  \overline\rho_f
  =\frac{\kzero^2}{2\pi r_e}(\delta_0+\ii\beta_0).
  \label{eq:mean-density-from-index}
\end{equation}
For a bulk unit cell of volume $V_{\mathrm{uc}}$, it can also be calculated as
\begin{equation}
  \overline\rho_f
  =\frac{1}{V_{\mathrm{uc}}}
  \sum_{j\in\mathrm{uc}}o_j
  \left[f_{j,\mathrm{WK}}^0(0)+f'_j+\ii f''_j\right].
  \label{eq:mean-density-from-cell}
\end{equation}
Thus Eqs.~\eqref{eq:mean-density-from-index} and
\eqref{eq:mean-density-from-cell} use exactly the same forward amplitudes as
Eq.~\eqref{eq:implemented-forward-factor}; no independent atomic-number
shortcut is present in either optical path.

Let $\Theta(-z)$ be one inside the substrate and zero in vacuum.  Splitting
the actual index into reference and perturbation parts,
\begin{equation}
  n^2(\bm r)=n_0^2(z)+\Delta n^2(\bm r),
\end{equation}
gives the corrected perturbation
\begin{align}
  \Delta n^2(\bm r)
  &=n^2(\bm r)-n_0^2(z)\nonumber\\
  &=-\frac{4\pi r_e}{\kzero^2}
  \left[
    \rho_f(\bm r)-\overline\rho_f\,\Theta(-z)
  \right].
  \label{eq:index-perturbation}
\end{align}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta n^2(\bm r)
  =-\frac{4\pi r_e}{\kzero^2}\rho_f(\bm r)
  +(2\delta_0+2\ii\beta_0)\Theta(-z).
  \label{eq:index-perturbation-expanded}
\end{equation}
The reference term is present only in the material half-space.  Treating it as
a constant throughout all space would incorrectly reduce it to a delta
function only at $\bm Q=0$ and would erase the specular surface contribution.

\section{Helmholtz equation and the Green function}

\subsection{Reference Helmholtz operator}

For a nonmagnetic medium, the electric field obeys
\begin{equation}
  \left[\nabla^2+\kzero^2n^2(\bm r)\right]\bm E(\bm r)=0.
  \label{eq:helmholtz}
\end{equation}
Define the reference operator
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_0
  =\nabla^2+\kzero^2n_0^2(\bm r).
  \label{eq:reference-operator}
\end{equation}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:index-perturbation}, the exact field satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_0\bm E(\bm r)
  =-\kzero^2\Delta n^2(\bm r)\bm E(\bm r).
  \label{eq:perturbed-helmholtz}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Green-function normalization and sign}

We define the outgoing dyadic Green function by
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
  \mathcal L_0\Gzero(\bm r,\bm r')
  =-\frac{\kzero^2}{\epsilon_0}
  \bm I\,\delta(\bm r-\bm r').
  }
  \label{eq:green-definition}
\end{equation}
Here $\epsilon_0$ is the vacuum permittivity and $\bm I$ is the identity
tensor.  The negative source sign is intentional.  With the Fourier and time
conventions above, it makes Eq.~\eqref{eq:green-definition} consistent with
both Eq.~\eqref{eq:perturbed-helmholtz} and the positive kernel in the
Lippmann--Schwinger equation below.

Other references sometimes define the Green function with the opposite point
source or define $\Delta n^2=n_0^2-n^2$.  Either choice is permissible, but
all subsequent signs must then change consistently.  The phase of the final
specular correction must be checked against a Fresnel limiting case because
it controls interference with the reference reflection amplitude.

\subsection{Far-field reciprocal form}

Let the observation point be in vacuum at distance $r$ in the outgoing
direction $\bm k_f$.  For an analyzed exit polarization
$\bm\epsilon_f$, reciprocity gives the selected-polarization action of the
Green tensor as
\begin{equation}
  \Gzero(\bm r,\bm r')\bm p
  \underset{r\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}
  \frac{\kzero^2\ee^{-\ii\kzero r}}
       {4\pi\epsilon_0 r}
  \bm\epsilon_f
  \left[
    \bm E_0(\bm r',-\bm k_f)
    \mathbin{\cdot}\bm p
  \right].
  \label{eq:green-far-field}
\end{equation}
The field $\bm E_0(\bm r',-\bm k_f)$ is the reciprocal exit field generated
by a plane wave incident from the detector direction with wavevector
$-\bm k_f$.  It is calculated in the same complex reference profile as the
incident field.  It is not complex conjugated; absorption does not invalidate
the reciprocity construction for the reciprocal, linear medium considered
here.

\section{Lippmann--Schwinger equation and Born series}

Let $\bm E_0(\bm r,\bm k_i)$ be the exact solution in the reference medium for
the incident wavevector $\bm k_i$.  Applying the Green function to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:perturbed-helmholtz} gives the exact integral equation
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
  \bm E(\bm r)
  =\bm E_0(\bm r)
  +\epsilon_0\int\Gzero(\bm r,\bm r')
  \Delta n^2(\bm r')\bm E(\bm r')\,\dd^3r'.
  }
  \label{eq:lippmann-schwinger}
\end{equation}
Iterating Eq.~\eqref{eq:lippmann-schwinger} produces the Born series:
\begin{align}
  \bm E(\bm r)
  ={}&\bm E_0(\bm r)\nonumber\\
  &+\epsilon_0\int\Gzero(\bm r,\bm r')
      \Delta n^2(\bm r')\bm E_0(\bm r')\,\dd^3r'\nonumber\\
  &+\epsilon_0^2\iint
      \Gzero(\bm r,\bm r')\Delta n^2(\bm r')
      \Gzero(\bm r',\bm r'')\Delta n^2(\bm r'')
      \bm E_0(\bm r'')\,\dd^3r'\dd^3r''\nonumber\\
  &+\cdots .
  \label{eq:born-series}
\end{align}
Each additional term adds another scattering event from the perturbation.
Reflection, transmission, refraction, and repeated reflection at the planar
interfaces are already included to all orders in $\bm E_0$ and $\Gzero$.
DWBA is ``Born'' only with respect to the residual contrast $\Delta n^2$.

First-order DWBA retains the first correction:
\begin{equation}
  \delta\bm E^{(1)}(\bm r)
  =\epsilon_0\int\Gzero(\bm r,\bm r')
  \Delta n^2(\bm r')
  \bm E_0(\bm r',\bm k_i)\,\dd^3r'.
  \label{eq:first-order-field}
\end{equation}
Substituting the far-field Green function from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:green-far-field} yields
\begin{align}
  \delta\bm E_f(\bm r)
  ={}&
  \frac{\kzero^2\ee^{-\ii\kzero r}}{4\pi r}
  \bm\epsilon_f\nonumber\\
  &\times
  \int
  \bm E_0(\bm r',-\bm k_f)
  \mathbin{\cdot}\bm E_0(\bm r',\bm k_i)
  \Delta n^2(\bm r')\,\dd^3r'.
  \label{eq:far-field-before-density}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:far-field-before-density} is the field-level DWBA master
equation.  Its squared matrix element gives the usual differential scattering
cross-section, but the equation itself retains the phase needed for coherent
addition to another outgoing field.

\section{Insertion of the refractive-index perturbation}

Inserting Eq.~\eqref{eq:index-perturbation} into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:far-field-before-density} cancels the factor
$\kzero^2/(4\pi)$:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
  \delta\bm E_f(\bm r)
  =-r_e\frac{\ee^{-\ii\kzero r}}{r}
  \bm\epsilon_f F_{\Delta,\mathrm{DWBA}}.
  }
  \label{eq:scattered-field-electron-units}
\end{equation}
The contrast DWBA amplitude in electron units is
\begin{align}
  F_{\Delta,\mathrm{DWBA}}
  ={}&\int
  \bm E_0(\bm r',-\bm k_f)
  \mathbin{\cdot}\bm E_0(\bm r',\bm k_i)\nonumber\\
  &\times
  \left[
    \rho_f(\bm r')-\overline\rho_f\Theta(-z')
  \right]\,\dd^3r'.
  \label{eq:contrast-dwba-definition}
\end{align}
It is useful to split this into two terms:
\begin{equation}
  F_{\Delta,\mathrm{DWBA}}
  =F_{\mathrm{atomic,DWBA}}-F_{\mathrm{mean,DWBA}},
  \label{eq:atomic-minus-mean}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
  F_{\mathrm{atomic,DWBA}}
  ={}&\int
  \bm E_0(\bm r',-\bm k_f)
  \mathbin{\cdot}\bm E_0(\bm r',\bm k_i)
  \rho_f(\bm r')\,\dd^3r',
  \label{eq:atomic-dwba-definition}\\
  F_{\mathrm{mean,DWBA}}
  ={}&\overline\rho_f\int_{z'<0}
  \bm E_0(\bm r',-\bm k_f)
  \mathbin{\cdot}\bm E_0(\bm r',\bm k_i)\,\dd^3r'.
  \label{eq:mean-dwba-definition}
\end{align}

For a finite scatterer and a unit incident field, the corresponding
differential cross-section is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\dd\sigma}{\dd\Omega}
  =r_e^2\left|F_{\Delta,\mathrm{DWBA}}\right|^2.
  \label{eq:dwba-cross-section}
\end{equation}
The local-field amplitudes and polarization contraction are already contained
in $F_{\Delta,\mathrm{DWBA}}$.  Equation~\eqref{eq:dwba-cross-section} shows
why an additional factor $\kzero^2/(16\pi^2)$ must not remain after converting
the dielectric perturbation into atomic electron units.

\section{Four Renaud channels and the orGUI amplitude}

In an optical medium $j$, both the incident and reciprocal exit fields contain
downward and upward components.  Let
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_i,\sigma_f\in\{+1,-1\}
\end{equation}
label those components.  orGUI orders the four channels as
\begin{equation}
  ++,\quad +-,\quad -+,\quad --.
\end{equation}
For channel $\nu=(\sigma_i,\sigma_f)$, the normal scattering transfer is
\begin{equation}
  Q_{z,j}^{\nu}
  =-\left(\sigma_i k_{z,j,i}+\sigma_f k_{z,j,f}\right).
  \label{eq:channel-Qz}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Polarization basis and amplitude normalization}

For every propagation branch, orGUI uses the right-handed Renaud basis
\begin{equation}
  \bm\epsilon_s^\sigma\mathbin{\times}\bm\epsilon_p^\sigma
  =\widehat{\bm k}^{\,\sigma}.
  \label{eq:right-handed-polarization}
\end{equation}
Reversing the normal propagation direction therefore reverses the
tangential component of the $p$-polarization vector.  This sign matters for
coherent amplitudes even though it cancels from an isolated intensity.

The transfer matrix is propagated internally in tangential-field variables,
which are continuous and numerically stable at interfaces.  Let
$T_{p,j}^{\pm}$ denote the downward and upward coefficients obtained by
decomposing that internal tangential field.  The public Renaud amplitudes are
\begin{equation}
  A_{p,j}^{+}=T_{p,j}^{+},
  \qquad
  A_{p,j}^{-}=-T_{p,j}^{-}.
  \label{eq:p-amplitude-conversion}
\end{equation}
For $s$ polarization no conversion is required.  In the incident vacuum,
$A^+=1$, and the public specular amplitude satisfies
$r_S=A^-$ for both $s$ and $p$.  In particular, $r_S$ is the coefficient of
the complete reflected Renaud polarization vector, not the ratio of the two
tangential $p$ components.

For a p-polarized branch in medium $j$, the vector reconstructed by the
native DWBA kernel is, apart from its propagation phase,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\bm E_{p,j}^{\sigma}}{E_{\mathrm{inc}}}
  =A_{p,j}^{\sigma}\sin\alpha
   \left(
     \sigma\widehat{\bm e}_{\parallel}
     -\frac{\kzero\cos\alpha}{k_{z,j}}\widehat{\bm e}_z
   \right).
  \label{eq:p-vector-reconstruction}
\end{equation}
This form obeys $\bm k^\sigma\mathbin{\cdot}\bm E_p^\sigma=0$.  The native
solver carries $A^\sigma/k_{z,j}$ alongside $A^\sigma$ and supplies the
analytic terminal-medium limit at $k_{z,j}=0$.  Correspondingly, a sampled
tangential field uses $A^+\exp(\ii k_z\Delta z)-
A^-\exp(-\ii k_z\Delta z)$ for $p$, whereas the two branches add for $s$.

Let $\varphi$ be the implemented in-plane angle between the incident and exit
geometries.  With the right-handed basis above, the bilinear reciprocal-field
contractions multiplying $A_i^{\sigma_i}A_f^{\sigma_f}$ are
\begin{align}
  P_{ss}^{\sigma_i\sigma_f}
    &=\cos\varphi,\nonumber\\
  P_{sp}^{\sigma_i\sigma_f}
    &=-\sigma_f\sin\alpha_f\sin\varphi,\nonumber\\
  P_{ps}^{\sigma_i\sigma_f}
    &=-\sigma_i\sin\alpha_i\sin\varphi,\nonumber\\
  P_{pp}^{\sigma_i\sigma_f}
    &=\sin\alpha_i\sin\alpha_f
      \left[
        \frac{\kzero^2\cos\alpha_i\cos\alpha_f}
             {k_{z,j,i}k_{z,j,f}}
        -\sigma_i\sigma_f\cos\varphi
      \right].
  \label{eq:implemented-polarization-contractions}
\end{align}
In vacuum, $k_{z,i}=-\kzero\sin\alpha_i$ and
$k_{z,f}=-\kzero\sin\alpha_f$, so the last line becomes
\begin{equation}
  P_{pp}^{\sigma_i\sigma_f}
  =\cos\alpha_i\cos\alpha_f
   -\sigma_i\sigma_f\sin\alpha_i\sin\alpha_f\cos\varphi,
\end{equation}
which, together with the first three lines, is the Renaud Born limit.  The
reciprocal exit amplitudes are used bilinearly as written; they are not
complex conjugated.  Complex conjugation occurs only when an intensity is
formed.

The channel coefficient is schematically
\begin{align}
  \mathcal C_{j,\nu}
  ={}&A_{j,i}^{\sigma_i}A_{j,f}^{\sigma_f}
  \left(
    \bm\epsilon_{j,f}^{\sigma_f}
    \mathbin{\cdot}
    \bm\epsilon_{j,i}^{\sigma_i}
  \right)\nonumber\\
  &\times
  \exp\!\left[
    \ii\left(
      \sigma_i k_{z,j,i}z_{j,i}^{\mathrm{ref}}
      +\sigma_f k_{z,j,f}z_{j,f}^{\mathrm{ref}}
    \right)
  \right].
  \label{eq:channel-coefficient}
\end{align}
The reference-position phase in Eq.~\eqref{eq:channel-coefficient} is required
because the stored amplitudes are local amplitudes referenced to
$z_{j}^{\mathrm{ref}}$ rather than to a global origin.

The atomic contribution can then be written
\begin{align}
  F_{\mathrm{atomic,DWBA}}
  =\sum_{\nu}\sum_a
  \mathcal C_{j(a),\nu}\,
  o_a f_a(Q_{j(a),\nu}^2)
  \ee^{-W_a(Q_{j(a),\nu}^2)}
  \ee^{\ii\bm Q_{j(a),\nu}\cdot\bm r_a},
  \label{eq:four-channel-atomic-amplitude}
\end{align}
including the coherent-domain sum when more than one domain is present.
Every channel generally has a different complex $Q_z$ and therefore a
different complex $Q^2$.

The bulk kernel includes the electric-field amplitudes, polarization, atomic
form factors, Debye--Waller factors, atomic coordinates, coherent domains,
and the bulk lattice sum.  For \texttt{bulk\_mode=unit\_cell}, and for every
non-specular point, \texttt{F\_DWBA} returns
$F_{\mathrm{atomic,DWBA}}$ in electrons per configured reference lateral
unit cell.  For a semi-infinite specular point it instead performs the
cellwise reference-density subtraction of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:contrast-bulk-cellwise} and returns
$F_{\Delta,\mathrm{DWBA}}$.  The distinction is intentional: the homogeneous
reference has no Fourier component at nonzero $\Qpar$, and a single-cell
quantity is not yet a half-space contrast integral.

For an atom in the terminal substrate,
\begin{equation}
  A_i^-=A_f^-=0.
\end{equation}
Only the $++$ channel survives, and its coefficient will be denoted simply by
$\mathcal C_{++}$.

\section{Semi-infinite bulk and the new
\texorpdfstring{$1/(\ii Q_z)$}{1/(i Qz)} term}

\subsection{Relation to the dynamical-theory
\texorpdfstring{$\chi_0$}{chi0} term}
\label{sec:chi-zero}

The subtraction derived in this section is not a bookkeeping convention.  It is
the DWBA restatement of a step that dynamical diffraction theory performs at
the very start, and the argument is clearest in that setting.

Dynamical theory expands the triply periodic susceptibility $\chi=n^2-1$ over
reciprocal lattice vectors $\bm H$,
\begin{equation}
  \chi(\bm r)=\sum_{\bm H}\chi_{\bm H}\ee^{\ii\bm H\cdot\bm r},
  \qquad
  \chi_{\bm H}
  =-\frac{4\pi r_e}{\kzero^2V_{\mathrm{uc}}}F_{\bm H},
  \label{eq:chi-fourier}
\end{equation}
so that by Eq.~\eqref{eq:index-squared} the $\bm H=\bm 0$ coefficient is
precisely the mean density of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mean-density-from-cell},
\begin{equation}
  \chi_0=-\frac{4\pi r_e}{\kzero^2}\overline\rho_f
        =-2\left(\delta_0+\ii\beta_0\right).
  \label{eq:chi-zero-value}
\end{equation}
Inserting the Bloch ansatz
$\bm E=\sum_{\bm H}\bm E_{\bm H}
\exp(-\ii\bm k_{\bm H}\mathbin{\cdot}\bm r)$
into Eq.~\eqref{eq:helmholtz} gives the fundamental equations of dynamical
theory,
\begin{equation}
  \left(k_{\bm H}^2-\kzero^2\right)\bm E_{\bm H}
  =\kzero^2\sum_{\bm H'}\chi_{\bm H-\bm H'}\bm E_{\bm H'} .
  \label{eq:dynamical-fundamental}
\end{equation}
The term $\bm H'=\bm H$ on the right-hand side carries the single coefficient
$\chi_0$ and couples no two distinct Fourier components.  It is therefore moved
to the left and absorbed into the propagation constant,
\begin{equation}
  K^2=\kzero^2\left(1+\chi_0\right),
  \qquad
  \left(k_{\bm H}^2-K^2\right)\bm E_{\bm H}
  =\kzero^2\sum_{\bm H'\neq\bm H}\chi_{\bm H-\bm H'}\bm E_{\bm H'} .
  \label{eq:dynamical-reduced}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:dynamical-reduced} contains the entire argument.  In
dynamical theory $\chi_0$ is a redefinition of the dispersion relation, not a
source term.  Refraction, the critical angle, absorption, and the Fresnel or
Parratt specular amplitude are all consequences of its left-hand side.  Only
the coefficients with $\bm H\neq\bm 0$ ever act as scattering.

The specular rod is the unique rod that contains the point $\bm H=\bm 0$.
Every other rod passes only through reciprocal lattice points with
$\bm H_{\parallel}\neq\bm 0$.  That is the structural reason for the two
consequences drawn from Eq.~\eqref{eq:half-space-transform} below, and it
explains why the published DWBA treatments of crystal truncation rods, which
work on non-specular rods, never need the subtraction.

\subsection{The exact Fresnel amplitude in first-Born form}
\label{sec:fresnel-first-born}

The one-beam limit of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dynamical-reduced}, in which the sum on the
right is empty, is solved exactly by matching tangential fields at the surface.
For a single interface the result rearranges into
\begin{align}
  r_0
  &=\frac{k_{z,0}-k_{z,1}}{k_{z,0}+k_{z,1}}
   =\frac{k_{z,0}^2-k_{z,1}^2}{\left(k_{z,0}+k_{z,1}\right)^2}\nonumber\\
  &=\frac{-\kzero^2\chi_0}{\left(k_{z,0}+k_{z,1}\right)^2}
   =\frac{4\pi r_e\overline\rho_f}{\left(k_{z,0}+k_{z,1}\right)^2},
  \label{eq:fresnel-first-born-form}
\end{align}
where $k_{z,1}^2=k_{z,0}^2+\kzero^2\chi_0$ was used.
Equation~\eqref{eq:fresnel-first-born-form} is exact, not perturbative.  It
states that the exact Fresnel amplitude is the first-Born answer with the
vacuum $Q_z$ replaced by the sum of the in-medium normal wavevectors.  For
$k_{z,1}\rightarrow k_{z,0}=\kappa_0$ the denominator becomes $Q_z^2$ and
\begin{equation}
  r_0\longrightarrow
  \frac{4\pi r_e\overline\rho_f}{Q_z^2}
  =\frac{\pi r_e\overline\rho_f}{\kappa_0^2},
  \label{eq:fresnel-high-angle}
\end{equation}
which is the limit used as a phase check in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fresnel-phase-check}.

\subsection{The mean-density term as the
\texorpdfstring{$\bm H=\bm 0$}{H=0} pole of the truncation sum}
\label{sec:h-zero-pole}

The same term can be exhibited directly inside the kinematical lattice sum,
which makes the $1/(\ii Q_z)$ factor a derived object rather than a
distributional identity.  The semi-infinite stacking sum used in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:atomic-bulk-sum} below,
\begin{equation}
  S(Q_z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\ee^{-\ii Q_zmc}
        =\frac{1}{1-\ee^{-\ii Q_zc}},
  \label{eq:stacking-sum}
\end{equation}
is a meromorphic function of the continuous rod coordinate
$L=Q_zc/(2\pi)$ with a simple pole at every integer $l$, that is, at every
reciprocal lattice point along the rod.  Differentiating the denominator gives
the residue of $F_{hkL}S$ at $L=l$ as
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname*{Res}_{L=l}
  \frac{F_{hkL}}{1-\ee^{-2\pi\ii L}}
  =\frac{F_{hkl}}{2\pi\ii}.
  \label{eq:rod-residue}
\end{equation}
Kaganer shows that the corresponding pole expansion is exact, and that each
pole term is the asymptotic two-beam dynamical amplitude of the reflection
$hkl$ far from its Bragg peak.  The kinematical crystal truncation rod is
therefore a coherent sum of dynamical Bragg tails.

On a non-specular rod every pole sits at a reciprocal lattice point with
$\bm H_{\parallel}\neq\bm 0$.  On the specular rod one pole sits at $l=0$, and
its residue is the total scattering power of the unit cell,
\begin{equation}
  F_{000}=\sum_{a\in\mathrm{uc}}o_af_{a,\mathrm{opt}}
         =V_{\mathrm{uc}}\overline\rho_f
         =A_{\mathrm{ref}}c\,\overline\rho_f .
  \label{eq:f000-residue}
\end{equation}
Isolating that single pole, with $2\pi L=Q_zc$, gives
\begin{equation}
  \frac{F_{000}}{2\pi\ii}\frac{1}{L}
  =\frac{F_{000}}{\ii Q_zc}
  =\frac{A_{\mathrm{ref}}\overline\rho_f}{\ii Q_z},
  \label{eq:l-zero-pole}
\end{equation}
which is exactly the mean-density factor obtained independently in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mean-bulk-sum} below.  Carrying
Eq.~\eqref{eq:l-zero-pole} through the plane-wave normalization of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:reflection-correction} yields
\begin{equation}
  \delta r_{\mathrm{mean}}
  =\frac{2\pi\ii r_e}{\kappa_0A_{\mathrm{ref}}}
   \frac{A_{\mathrm{ref}}\overline\rho_f}{\ii Q_z}
  =\frac{2\pi r_e\overline\rho_f}{\kappa_0Q_z}
  =\frac{4\pi r_e\overline\rho_f}{Q_z^2},
  \label{eq:pole-equals-fresnel}
\end{equation}
identical to the high-angle Fresnel amplitude of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fresnel-high-angle}.

The relation is therefore an identity, not an analogy.  The $1/(\ii Q_z)$ term
is the $\bm H=\bm 0$ ``Bragg reflection'' of the crystal, and the
$\bm H=\bm 0$ Bragg reflection is specular reflection.  Dynamical theory
disposes of it by absorbing $\chi_0$ into the refractive index, as in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dynamical-reduced}.  Kinematical theory disposes of it by leaving
it inside the lattice sum.  Exactly one of the two must be done.  Because $r_0$
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:total-reflection-amplitude} is taken from the exact layered
solution, the first route has already been chosen, and the term must be removed
from the second.

Two practical consequences follow.  First, well above the critical angle and
away from Bragg peaks $\delta r_{\mathrm{mean}}\simeq r_0$, so an unsubtracted
specular rod returns $r_{\mathrm{total}}\simeq2r_0$ and a reflectivity too
large by roughly a factor of four.  This is a sharper diagnostic than visual
inspection of a fit.  Second, as $Q_z\rightarrow0$ the kinematical specular sum
diverges like $A_{\mathrm{ref}}\overline\rho_f/(\ii Q_z)$, whereas the true
reflectivity saturates at $R=1$ below the critical angle.  The kinematical
specular rod is thus unphysical at small $Q_z$ irrespective of any surface
model.  Removing the divergent pole and replacing it by the bounded exact $r_0$
is what repairs this.

\subsection{Why a truncated constant is not only a point at
\texorpdfstring{$\bm Q=0$}{Q=0}}

For a constant extending over all three-dimensional space,
\begin{equation}
  \int_{\mathbb R^3}C\ee^{\ii\bm Q\cdot\bm r}\,\dd^3r
  =(2\pi)^3C\,\delta^{(3)}(\bm Q).
\end{equation}
The reference density, however, exists only for $z<0$.  Its transform
factorizes as
\begin{align}
  &\int\dd^2r_{\parallel}\,
  \ee^{\ii\Qpar\cdot\rpar}
  \int_{-\infty}^{0}\dd z\,
  C\ee^{\ii Q_z z}\nonumber\\
  &\qquad=(2\pi)^2\delta^{(2)}(\Qpar)
  \frac{C}{\ii Q_z},
  \label{eq:half-space-transform}
\end{align}
where the complex DWBA $Q_z$ supplies physical convergence through absorption.
For a real $Q_z$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:half-space-transform} is understood with the
usual infinitesimal convergence factor and corresponding distributional
limit.

Equation~\eqref{eq:half-space-transform} has two immediate consequences:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item For $\Qpar\neq\bm 0$, the laterally homogeneous reference term is
  zero.  The existing atomic \texttt{F\_DWBA} is therefore already the
  contrast amplitude for a non-specular CTR.
  \item For $\Qpar=\bm 0$, the reference contributes along the complete
  specular rod.  It cannot be discarded as a term only at $Q_z=0$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Unit-cell and lattice-sum derivation}

Choose the top bulk unit cell to occupy $-c\leq z<0$, where $c>0$ is the
surface-normal repeat length, and let $A_{\mathrm{ref}}$ be its configured
reference lateral area.  Define the atomic unit-cell factor, before applying
the $++$ field coefficient, by
\begin{equation}
  F_{\mathrm{uc}}^{\mathrm{atomic}}(Q_z)
  =\sum_{a\in\mathrm{uc}}
  o_a f_a(Q^2)\ee^{-W_a(Q^2)}
  \ee^{\ii\bm Q\cdot\bm r_a}.
  \label{eq:atomic-unit-cell-factor}
\end{equation}
The atomic semi-infinite bulk amplitude is
\begin{equation}
  F_{\mathrm{atomic,DWBA}}
  =\mathcal C_{++}
  \frac{F_{\mathrm{uc}}^{\mathrm{atomic}}(Q_z)}
       {1-\ee^{-\ii Q_zc}}.
  \label{eq:atomic-bulk-sum}
\end{equation}

The mean-density contribution from the same top unit cell is
\begin{align}
  F_{\mathrm{mean,uc}}(Q_z)
  &=A_{\mathrm{ref}}\overline\rho_f
    \int_{-c}^{0}\ee^{\ii Q_z z}\,\dd z\nonumber\\
  &=A_{\mathrm{ref}}\overline\rho_f
    \frac{1-\ee^{-\ii Q_zc}}{\ii Q_z}.
  \label{eq:mean-cell-factor}
\end{align}
Applying the same semi-infinite lattice sum gives
\begin{align}
  F_{\mathrm{mean,DWBA}}
  &=\mathcal C_{++}
    \frac{F_{\mathrm{mean,uc}}(Q_z)}
         {1-\ee^{-\ii Q_zc}}\nonumber\\
  &=\boxed{
    \mathcal C_{++}
    \frac{A_{\mathrm{ref}}\overline\rho_f}{\ii Q_z}
    }.
  \label{eq:mean-bulk-sum}
\end{align}
The cancellation between numerator and denominator is the unit-cell version
of the half-space transform in Eq.~\eqref{eq:half-space-transform}.

Combining Eqs.~\eqref{eq:atomic-bulk-sum} and
\eqref{eq:mean-bulk-sum} gives the required contrast amplitude:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
  F_{\Delta,\mathrm{DWBA}}
  =\mathcal C_{++}
  \left[
    \frac{F_{\mathrm{uc}}^{\mathrm{atomic}}(Q_z)}
         {1-\ee^{-\ii Q_zc}}
    -\frac{A_{\mathrm{ref}}\overline\rho_f}{\ii Q_z}
  \right],
  \qquad \Qpar=\bm 0.
  }
  \label{eq:contrast-bulk-separated}
\end{equation}
An equivalent cellwise form is
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
  F_{\Delta,\mathrm{DWBA}}
  =\mathcal C_{++}
  \frac{
    F_{\mathrm{uc}}^{\mathrm{atomic}}(Q_z)
    -A_{\mathrm{ref}}\overline\rho_f
     \dfrac{1-\ee^{-\ii Q_zc}}{\ii Q_z}
  }{1-\ee^{-\ii Q_zc}}.
  }
  \label{eq:contrast-bulk-cellwise}
\end{equation}

The cellwise form makes the cancellation of the cell-average density explicit.
As $Q_z\rightarrow0$,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1-\ee^{-\ii Q_zc}}{\ii Q_z}\longrightarrow c,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  F_{\mathrm{uc}}^{\mathrm{atomic}}(0)
  =A_{\mathrm{ref}}c\,\overline\rho_f.
\end{equation}
Thus the leading mean-density contribution cancels before the lattice sum is
formed.  Numerically, both the lattice denominator and the finite-cell mean
factor should be evaluated with complex \texttt{expm1}-type expressions to
avoid cancellation error near $Q_zc=0$.

The relation implemented by the semi-infinite specular bulk API is therefore
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
  F_{\mathrm{DWBA}}^{\mathrm{semi\mbox{-}infinite,specular}}
  =F_{\mathrm{atomic,DWBA}}
  -\mathcal C_{++}
   \frac{A_{\mathrm{ref}}\overline\rho_f}{\ii Q_z}
  =F_{\Delta,\mathrm{DWBA}},
  \qquad \Qpar=\bm 0.
  }
  \label{eq:current-minus-new-term}
\end{equation}

The native kernel receives the complete refractive-index array stored in the
prepared incident field, and preparation requires the reciprocal exit field
to contain an identical array and identical reference planes.  It derives
$\overline\rho_f$ from the terminal value of that frozen profile by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mean-density-from-index}; it does not recalculate the index from
the atomic parameters at evaluation time.  Consequently, changing live atomic
occupancies after preparation changes the perturbation but cannot silently
change $n_0$.  A graded reference changes the channel amplitudes and normal
wavevectors in $\mathcal C_{++}$, while the terminal homogeneous half-space
still has the analytic $1/(\ii Q_z)$ transform.  Finite surface and graded
interface contributions can therefore be added separately without changing
the terminal bulk integral.

\subsection{Truncation of the atomic densities at the surface plane}

Equation~\eqref{eq:index-perturbation} subtracts a reference density that is
sharply truncated at $z=0$, whereas $\rho_f(\bm r)$ is a superposition of
smooth atomic densities.  The cancellation in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:contrast-bulk-cellwise} is therefore exact only if the electron
density of the top unit cell is contained entirely in $z<0$.  If the surface
plane bisects an atom, a residual contrast of the order of the truncated
electron fraction of that atom survives at all $Q_z$, because the atomic term
retains the part of the cloud at $z>0$ while the reference term does not.

Kaganer reports the same effect when comparing kinematical and dynamical CTR
calculations: the residual limits the achievable agreement to roughly
$10^{-7}$ of the peak intensity, and it is removed by choosing the cell origin
so that the mathematical surface falls between atomic layers rather than
through one.  For GaAs(001) this means placing the atoms at fractional depths
$1/8$, $3/8$, $5/8$, $7/8$ rather than $0$, $1/4$, $1/2$, $3/4$.

The consequence here is that the bulk cell used to generate
$\overline\rho_f$ and the bulk cell used to generate
$F_{\mathrm{uc}}^{\mathrm{atomic}}$ must share one origin, chosen to minimise
atomic truncation at $z=0$.  Invariant~8 below states the corresponding test.

\section{Observable specular field}

Equation~\eqref{eq:scattered-field-electron-units} describes spherical
far-field radiation from a finite coherent scattering volume.  An ideal
laterally infinite specular interface instead produces a plane reflected
wave.  The lateral integral supplies $\delta^{(2)}(\Qpar)$, and the result must
be normalized as a dimensionless reflection coefficient rather than by
$\ee^{-\ii\kzero r}/r$.

Let
\begin{equation}
  \kappa_0=\kzero\sin\alpha=-k_{z,0}>0
\end{equation}
be the positive magnitude of the vacuum normal wavevector.  For the Fourier
phase in Eq.~\eqref{eq:fourier-convention}, the plane-wave correction per
reference lateral cell is
\begin{equation}
  \delta r_{\mathrm{DWBA}}
  =\frac{2\pi\ii r_e}{\kappa_0}
   \frac{F_{\Delta,\mathrm{DWBA}}}{A_{\mathrm{ref}}}.
  \label{eq:reflection-correction}
\end{equation}
The prefactor has the required dimensions:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{r_e}{\kappa_0}
  \frac{F}{A}
  \sim
  \frac{\text{Angstrom}}{\text{Angstrom}^{-1}}
  \frac{\text{electrons}}{\text{Angstrom}^2},
\end{equation}
which is dimensionless when electron units are understood for $F$.

\subsection{Comparison with the published kinematical prefactor}
\label{sec:prefactor-check}

The prefactor in Eq.~\eqref{eq:reflection-correction} can be checked against an
independently derived semi-infinite kinematical amplitude.  Kaganer, in the
work listed in Section~\ref{sec:references}, gives the plane-wave amplitude per
lateral cell as
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{kin}}(hkL)
  =\frac{r_e\lambda}{a^2\gamma_f}
   \frac{\ii F_{hkL}}{\exp(2\pi\ii L)-1},
  \label{eq:kaganer-kinematical}
\end{equation}
where $a^2$ is the lateral cell area, $\gamma_f=\sin\alpha_f$ is the sine of
the exit angle, and $2\pi L=Q_zc$.  Substituting $\lambda=2\pi/\kzero$ and
$\kappa_f=\kzero\gamma_f$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \frac{r_e\lambda}{a^2\gamma_f}
  =\frac{2\pi r_e}{\kappa_f A_{\mathrm{ref}}},
\end{equation}
so that Eq.~\eqref{eq:kaganer-kinematical} becomes
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{kin}}
  =\frac{2\pi\ii r_e}{\kappa_f}
   \frac{1}{A_{\mathrm{ref}}}
   \frac{F_{hkL}}{\exp(\ii Q_zc)-1}.
\end{equation}
This is Eq.~\eqref{eq:reflection-correction} with the atomic lattice sum
inserted.  The agreement is exact, including the factor $\ii$ and the absence
of any residual $\kzero^2/(4\pi)$, and it corroborates invariant~1 below
against a result that has itself been benchmarked against multi-beam dynamical
diffraction theory.

Two conventions differ harmlessly but must be respected.  First, the lattice
denominator $\exp(\ii Q_zc)-1$ and the denominator $1-\exp(-\ii Q_zc)$ used
here differ by one factor $\exp(-\ii Q_zc)$, that is, by a shift of the
unit-cell origin by one repeat.  Because $\delta r_{\mathrm{DWBA}}$ is added
coherently to $r_0$, this phase is not free: the origin used for
$F_{\mathrm{uc}}^{\mathrm{atomic}}$, for the $\Theta(-z)$ step, and for $r_0$
must be one and the same.  Second, the normalizing wavevector in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:kaganer-kinematical} is the \emph{exit} normal component
$\kappa_f$.  On the specular rod $\kappa_f=\kappa_0$ and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:reflection-correction} is correct as written, but the same
normalization must not be reused off-specular without replacing $\kappa_0$ by
$\kappa_f$.

As a phase check, take a vacuum reference and a homogeneous half-space of
scattering-factor density $\rho_f$.  Then
\begin{equation}
  \frac{F}{A}=\frac{\rho_f}{\ii Q_z},
  \qquad Q_z=2\kappa_0,
\end{equation}
and Eq.~\eqref{eq:reflection-correction} gives
\begin{equation}
  \delta r
  =\frac{\pi r_e\rho_f}{\kappa_0^2},
  \label{eq:fresnel-phase-check}
\end{equation}
which is the high-angle Fresnel amplitude to first order, in agreement with
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fresnel-high-angle}.  The check is asymptotic: by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fresnel-first-born-form} it holds only for
$k_{z,1}\rightarrow k_{z,0}$, that is for $Q_z$ well above the critical
wavevector, and it must not be evaluated near $\alpha_c$.  This limit should
be retained as an executable regression test when the specular correction is
implemented.

For the actual DWBA decomposition, the total reflected amplitude is
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
  r_{\mathrm{total}}(\alpha)
  =r_0(\alpha)+\delta r_{\mathrm{DWBA}}(\alpha),
  }
  \label{eq:total-reflection-amplitude}
\end{equation}
where $r_0$ must come from the same prepared optical reference used to compute
$F_{\Delta,\mathrm{DWBA}}$.  In the current architecture this is the complex
amplitude \texttt{prepared.field\_i.r\_S}, not the square root of a separately
calculated reflectivity.

The coherent observable reflectivity is
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mathrm{coh}}=\left|r_0+\delta r_{\mathrm{DWBA}}\right|^2.
  \label{eq:coherent-reflectivity}
\end{equation}
A formally first-order expansion of the intensity would retain only
\begin{equation}
  R^{(1)}
  =|r_0|^2+2\operatorname{Re}
   \left[r_0^*\delta r_{\mathrm{DWBA}}\right].
  \label{eq:first-order-reflectivity}
\end{equation}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:coherent-reflectivity} additionally includes the
$|\delta r_{\mathrm{DWBA}}|^2$ term, which is second order in the perturbation.
First-order DWBA does not in general enforce exact energy conservation.  In
addition, absorption permits $R<1$ below the critical angle, and roughness can
transfer energy from the coherent specular beam into diffuse scattering.

For unpolarized radiation, $s$ and $p$ intensities are averaged incoherently:
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mathrm{unpolarized}}
  =\frac{1}{2}\left(|r_s|^2+|r_p|^2\right).
\end{equation}
The $s$ and $p$ amplitudes must not be added coherently.

\section{Absorption and empirical attenuation}

The normal transfer $Q_z$ is generally complex because $k_{z,i}$ and
$k_{z,f}$ are calculated from complex refractive indices.  For $z<0$, the
factor
\begin{equation}
  \ee^{\ii Q_z z}
\end{equation}
therefore already contains the exact incident-plus-exit attenuation described
by the reference DWBA fields.  A separate factor such as
$\exp[z/\Lambda(\alpha_i,\alpha_f)]$ would double-count absorption if used at
the same time.

An explicit empirical attenuation remains meaningful for a purely
kinematical comparison in which $Q_z$ is deliberately kept real.  If used, it
must be applied continuously both within the top unit cell and between bulk
repeats.  For the exact DWBA path it should default to zero.

\section{Implementation interpretation and validation invariants}

The implementation keeps three quantities distinct:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $F_{\mathrm{atomic,DWBA}}$: the atomic matrix element returned by
  \texttt{F\_DWBA} for a unit cell and at non-specular points;
  \item $F_{\Delta,\mathrm{DWBA}}$: the semi-infinite specular matrix element
  returned by \texttt{F\_DWBA} after subtraction of the terminal optical
  reference density;
  \item $\delta r_{\mathrm{DWBA}}$: the dimensionless specular reflection
  correction after plane-wave normalization, assembled by
  \texttt{specular\_DWBA\_amplitude}.
\end{enumerate}
The convenience function \texttt{specular\_DWBA\_reflectivity} returns either
Eq.~\eqref{eq:coherent-reflectivity} or the formal first-order intensity of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:first-order-reflectivity}; its unpolarized mode averages the
$s$ and $p$ intensities incoherently.

The following invariants are suitable for regression tests:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Dimensional prefactor.} Substituting
  $\Delta n^2=-4\pi r_e\Delta\rho_f/\kzero^2$ into the DWBA field must leave
  the Thomson factor $r_e$, not $r_e\kzero/(4\pi)$.
  \item \textbf{Identical actual and reference media.} If the actual density
  equals the homogeneous reference density, then
  $F_{\Delta,\mathrm{DWBA}}=0$ and $r_{\mathrm{total}}=r_0$.
  \item \textbf{Off-specular invariance.} For $\Qpar\neq\bm0$, adding the
  mean-density subtraction must not change the existing CTR amplitude.
  \item \textbf{Low-$Q_z$ cancellation.} The atomic cell average and the
  continuum cell factor must cancel without catastrophic numerical loss.
  \item \textbf{High-angle Fresnel limit.} A homogeneous half-space treated
  relative to vacuum must reproduce the first-order Fresnel reflection
  amplitude, including its complex phase.
  \item \textbf{Prepared-reference consistency.} The $r_0$ added to
  $\delta r_{\mathrm{DWBA}}$ must be taken from the same prepared refractive
  profile and polarization state as the DWBA fields.
  \item \textbf{No duplicate attenuation.} Complex $Q_z$ and explicit
  empirical attenuation must not both describe the same absorption.
  \item \textbf{Forward-factor identity.} Optical profiles and all structure
  factors must use Eq.~\eqref{eq:implemented-forward-factor}, including for
  ionic species; replacing its Waasmaier--Kirfel limit by an exact $Z$ in
  only one path breaks the reference/perturbation cancellation.
  \item \textbf{Renaud p-amplitude sign.} The public upward p amplitude must
  obey Eq.~\eqref{eq:p-amplitude-conversion}, and all four channel
  contractions must reproduce
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:implemented-polarization-contractions} for nonzero complex
  $A_i^\pm$ and $A_f^\pm$.
  \item \textbf{Surface truncation of atomic densities.} Shifting the bulk cell
  origin so that the surface plane no longer bisects an atom must not change
  $F_{\Delta,\mathrm{DWBA}}$ by more than the level set by the truncated
  electron fraction.  The same origin must be used for
  $F_{\mathrm{uc}}^{\mathrm{atomic}}$, for $\overline\rho_f\Theta(-z)$, and for
  the reference amplitude $r_0$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{References}
\label{sec:references}

The Green-function and reciprocal-field construction follows
G.~Renaud, R.~Lazzari, and F.~Leroy, ``Probing surface and interface
morphology with grazing incidence small angle X-ray scattering,''
\emph{Surface Science Reports} \textbf{64} (2009), 255--380, especially
Sections~5.2--5.4.  The notation in this design note has been adapted to the
signed $k_z$, $A^\pm$, electric-field, and uppercase-$Q$ conventions used by
orGUI.

The works below were used as external cross-checks; the companion note
\texttt{dwba\_\allowbreak literature\_\allowbreak crosscheck.md} records the
comparison in detail.  No
published treatment of the mean-density subtraction on the \emph{specular} rod
was found.  The DWBA crystal-truncation-rod literature works on non-specular
rods, where Eq.~\eqref{eq:half-space-transform} makes the term vanish, and the
specular reflectivity literature works with continuum density profiles, where
the crystalline lattice sum never appears.  The combination therefore has to be
validated numerically rather than by citation.

\begin{itemize}
  \item G.~H.~Vineyard, ``Grazing-incidence diffraction and the distorted-wave
  approximation for the study of surfaces,'' \emph{Phys.\ Rev.\ B} \textbf{26}
  (1982), 4146--4159.  Origin of the surface DWBA: the reference medium carries
  the average polarizability of the crystal.
  \item S.~K.~Sinha, E.~B.~Sirota, S.~Garoff, and H.~B.~Stanley, ``X-ray and
  neutron scattering from rough surfaces,'' \emph{Phys.\ Rev.\ B} \textbf{38}
  (1988), 2297--2311.  Perturbation defined as the difference from the ideal
  reference profile, and precedent for a first-order DWBA correction added to
  the specular channel.
  \item V.~M.~Kaganer, ``Crystal truncation rods in kinematical and dynamical
  x-ray diffraction theories,'' \emph{Phys.\ Rev.\ B} \textbf{75} (2007),
  245425.  Source of Eq.~\eqref{eq:kaganer-kinematical}, of the four-channel
  DWBA sum with complex per-channel $Q_z$, and of the atomic-truncation
  caveat.
  \item V.~Hol\'y and P.~F.~Fewster, ``Crystal truncation rod X-ray
  scattering: exact dynamical calculation,'' \emph{J.\ Appl.\ Cryst.}
  \textbf{41} (2008), 18--26.  Slab decomposition with a Fresnel
  reflection/transmission matrix; the natural numerical benchmark for
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:contrast-bulk-separated} on the specular rod.
  \item A.~Caticha, ``Diffraction of x rays at the far tails of the Bragg
  peaks,'' \emph{Phys.\ Rev.\ B} \textbf{47} (1993), 76--83, and ``\ldots
  II.~Darwin dynamical theory,'' \emph{Phys.\ Rev.\ B} \textbf{49} (1994),
  33--38.  Continuous connection between the specular rod and the Fresnel
  reflectivity.
  \item U.~Pietsch, V.~Hol\'y, and T.~Baumbach, \emph{High-Resolution X-Ray
  Scattering: From Thin Films to Lateral Nanostructures}, Springer (2004), and
  V.~Hol\'y and T.~Baumbach, \emph{Phys.\ Rev.\ B} \textbf{49} (1994), 10668.
  DWBA with the perturbation taken relative to the laterally averaged
  susceptibility.
  \item X.-L.~Zhou and S.-H.~Chen, ``Theoretical foundation of X-ray and
  neutron reflectometry,'' \emph{Phys.\ Rep.} \textbf{257} (1995), 223--348.
  One-dimensional specular DWBA relative to a reference profile.
\end{itemize}
